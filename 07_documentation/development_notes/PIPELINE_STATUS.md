**Update 2026-08-25 (improvement batch; branch `OSP-boat-count-incorporation`, CODED AND STATIC-CHECKED, NOT RUN).** Eight requested improvements, plus two defects found during the review that fall inside items 1 and 2. Both Stan models parse under stanc 2.32.2 (matching the pinned rstan 2.32); every R module and both drivers parse; 44 unit assertions on the new logic pass (`06_diagnostics/test_improvements_2026-08-25.R`). **Nothing here is confirmed by a fitted run.** Ordered by how much each can move the number.

**(A) DEFECT, inference-changing: the shore I/E likelihood was on the wrong unit.** The shore I/E stream asserts `IE_obs ~ lognormal(log(lambda_E * L), sigma_IE)`. That was correct while shore ran on crabber-hours: `lambda_E` is crabbers present and `L` was `L_effective` in HOURS. The v7.7 move to gear-deployments replaced `L` with `tau_shore` (a dimensionless turnover, ~1.7) but left the OBSERVATION as `ie_crabber_hours`, so from v7.7 until now the model has been comparing crabber-HOURS against a predicted `lambda_E * tau` = crabber-TRIPS. On a typical WDF20 survey day that is ~331 observed against ~80 predicted, a ~4x scale mismatch the gear-count stream then has to absorb. **This is very likely the mechanism behind the standing, unexplained shore all-gear `sigma_IE` ~ 1.07 (GR-9), which was previously described only as "the two streams disagree by ~e^1 at the tails".** The boat stream was already correct (F2 put it on trips x turnover). Fixed by making the observation follow the effort unit through `bss_effort_spec()$ie_obs_col` (deployments -> `ie_trips`, the crabber arrival count already computed and previously unused; time units -> `ie_crabber_hours`), in both preps, so the pair cannot drift again. `ie_shore_obs_unit = "crabber_hours"` reproduces the old behaviour. **This MOVES the shore number and is the single change in this batch that should be isolated in its own run**, against the 2026-08-04 production baseline. Two consequences to watch: the shore I/E stream becomes genuinely informative about `tau_shore` (as the boat's is about `tau_boat`) instead of fighting the gear counts, and `sigma_IE` should fall sharply if the diagnosis is right. If it does not, the GR-9 tension is something else and the item stays open.

**(B) DEFECT: shore monthly PE effort share still on crabber-hours.** `pe_monthly_effort_share.R` computed shore daily effort as `mean_count * crabbers_per_gear * day_length`, the pre-v7.7 formula, with `day_length` being the seasonal `L_effective` regression. That silently re-weighted the shore monthly PE distribution toward long-day summer months. Component and port TOTALS were never affected (the share is normalised), but the monthly split was, and the monthly split is what a PE-fallback component's monthly tables and plots are built from. Now routed through `bss_effort_spec()` like the PE and the BSS.

**(1) Gear deployments used throughout, and documented.** Audited every place effort is formed, not just the CPUE denominator. Beyond (A) and (B): the fishery-opener spillover diagnostic measured CPUE per crabber-hour (the denominator this pipeline's own linearity test rejects, `beta_h` 0.57 against 1.05) and now uses `number_of_gear`; the gear-resolved daily-combined series applied the shore crabber-hours formula to the BOAT as well, using `crabbers_per_gear` against a trailer count, and now mirrors `run_pe_gear()` exactly including the crab fraction; the by-gear incomplete-trip bias, previously only ever measured in crabber-hours, is now reported in deployments with the crabber-hour figure retained; the BSS effort plot axis said "Crabber-hours"; the `bss_L_effective_*.csv` `L_prior_mu` column reported the hours regression even when the fit's `L` was a turnover, and is now `L_prior_center` + `L_unit` + a separate hours column. **Terminology:** "pot lift" is struck from both model documents. A deployment is a piece of gear a crabber had in the water on that trip (`number_of_gear`); a crabber who sets four pots and checks each three times contributes four deployments, not twelve, and slot re-use across the day is carried separately by `tau`. The old wording implied effort scales with checks, which the model does not assume. **Honest residual, now on-page:** deployments are the right unit but not a perfect one. The saturation exponent is 0.22 (shore all-gear), 0.27 (pot closure) and 0.25 (boat), and all three intervals exclude one AND zero, so all three still raise the saturation flag every run. A soak-time term would close the rest; the deployment unit gets most of the way.

**(2) Effective day length documented for what it actually is.** Production does not use a day length. Since v7.7 both components expand on a TURNOVER (`tau_shore` ~1.7, `tau_boat` ~1.2); `L_effective` (~5.26 h) is computed every run but sets only the `day_length` diagnostic column. Both quantities come from the same 15-minute presence series (`L_effective` = crabber-hours / peak present; turnover = arrivals / peak present), and their ratio is the implied trip length, 5.26 / 1.72 = 3.07 h against an interview-reported 3.23 h, which is the method's free internal consistency check and is now stated in both documents. Stale `L = 24` comments removed from `run_config.R` and `bss_day_length.R` (the flat 24-hour boat soak died at POOL-3 / v7.6). The known residual is coverage, not method: only 6 shore I/E surveys fall inside the season and they skew low-effort (mean percentile 0.27, rank test p = 0.06) against Pollock et al. (1997)'s implied ~40. Field item, not code.

**(3) Weekend is Saturday and Sunday.** `days_wkend` no longer includes Friday. Within month, paired across the 12 months carrying both day types: shore log(Fri/Sat) = -0.844 (Friday = 0.43x Saturday), t = -8.88, p = 2.4e-06; boat -0.507 (0.60x), t = -3.81, p = 0.003; against Monday-Thursday, Friday is 1.21x (p = 0.10) shore and 0.99x (p = 0.96) boat. Pooling it dragged the fitted weekend multiplier from 2.34x to 1.74x (shore) and 1.81x to 1.42x (boat), and moving it to the weekday stratum cuts the residual variance the AR must absorb by 15% (shore) / 5% (boat). **Known residual, deliberately not fixed here:** the boat's Sunday mean (3.4) sits BELOW its weekday mean (6.9), so grouping Sunday with Saturday is not obviously right for that stream. That rests on 5 sampled Sundays out of 50 boat days. Splitting Sunday out is a field-plan change (a shift change), not a config edit; see Section 6 and the new Tier-2 item.

**(4) Other fishery openers, auto-selected as effort covariates.** The razor-dig-only `B3` term is generalised into a `K_open` / `X_open` / `B_open` block in BOTH Stan models, with per-population candidate lists, so any opener can enter either population's effort model. `K_open = 0` is production and reproduces the previous model exactly; a razor-only run is mathematically identical to the retired `B3`. `opener_covariate_mode = "auto"` includes a candidate only if the spillover diagnostic's day-type + month ADJUSTED effect on that population's effort series clears `opener_auto_p` **after a multiplicity adjustment across the eight effort tests** (Benjamini-Hochberg by default), and only if the indicator is identifiable inside the fit's own window. Two things are stated in the code and the docs rather than assumed: selecting on a p-value from the data that then fits the term inflates the coefficient and understates its interval, so "auto" is a SCREEN and the arbiter is the effort-stream `elpd_loo` against an opener-free run; and the project has the worked example, razor-dig -> shore effort at p = 0.045, fitted as `B3` in Run 3, no predictive gain on any stream. The unit tests confirm the default behaves correctly on that known case: under BH over the eight effort tests razor does NOT survive while MA2 halibut -> boat trailers (p = 3.6e-06) does; with `opener_auto_p_adjust = "none"` razor would have been selected. CPUE gets no opener covariate (all eight opener-vs-catch-rate tests were null, p = 0.29 to 0.90). **The interaction that must not be missed:** boat effort counts ALL private boats, so a halibut term legitimately fits that series, but those boats are not crabbing, and a CONSTANT `f` converts the fitted surge to crab effort at the same rate as a closed-fishery day. Fitting the surge better while multiplying it by a flat `f` makes the boat MORE biased on opener days. `crab_fraction_strata` gained `"opener"` / `"month_opener"` for exactly this, and both drivers warn when a boat opener is active without them.

**(5) Incomplete trips: a four-arm diagnostic, production unchanged.** `sensitivity_incomplete_trips.csv` now reports `exclude` (production), `gear_only`, `impute_mean_cpue` and `keep`, each against the production arm, with the gear ratio under each and a length-bias test. **Direct answer to the question asked: no, option 2 is NOT already how the model functions.** `prep_bss_crab_*()` derives `intA` (the `R_G` / `R_G_boat` interview set) from the ALREADY-FILTERED frame, so an incomplete trip's fully observed gear count is discarded along with its truncated catch. Worse, the PE and the BSS disagree about it: `run_pe_*()` computes the boat gear-per-group from the UNFILTERED interview set, so the boat PE already behaves like `gear_only` while the boat BSS behaves like `exclude`. The diagnostic surfaces that. On option 1: applying mean CPUE to incomplete trips is a **pooled no-op by arithmetic**, not by luck, since a ratio-of-sums over complete-plus-imputed returns the complete-trip rate identically (confirmed by unit test); any movement is thin-stratum shrinkage toward the pooled rate, and in the BSS the same idea would be worse than a no-op because it would add fabricated observations that tighten the CPUE interval without adding information. The structural framing that bounds all of this is now written down: **effort does not come from interviews**, it comes from the gear and trailer/OSP counts, so no treatment of incomplete trips can add effort to the expansion; every arm acts through the catch rate or the gear ratios and nothing else. The one risk to `gear_only` is length-biased sampling (intercepted trips over-represent long trips; if long trips carry more gear, folding them into `R_G` biases the expansion up), which is why the Welch test is reported.

**(6) `bss_min_interviews` 20 -> 15.** The only component this changes is the private-boat POT CLOSURE (17 interviews), which was previously never fitted and entered the port total as an interval-free PE point; with the commercial/charter census that meant ~18% of the port total carried no uncertainty at all. Shore all-gear (2,741), shore pot-closure (856) and boat all-gear (145) are far above either threshold, so nothing else moves. Two things came with it. First, a SECOND floor `bss_min_interviews_fitted`, applied AFTER the pipeline drops zero-denominator rows and incomplete trips: the pre-existing guard counts UNFILTERED interviews, so it reads looser than what the likelihood sees, which was harmless with hundreds to spare and is not harmless at 15. Second, both floors were moved out of each driver's `params_model` into `run_config.R`, because `params_model` is merged ON TOP of `run_config` and was silently overriding it. **Honest expectation: 17 interviews over 76 days with ~44 effort counts is very thin and this fit may still fail the gate and fall back to PE.** That is a better outcome than before, because the failure becomes a measured gate verdict with per-fit diagnostics rather than a threshold decision taken before any data were looked at.

**(7) AR resolution escalation ladder (`ar_escalate`, ships FALSE).** With it on, each component starts at the FINEST rung (daily, ignoring `ar_max_resolution`), goes through the SAME convergence gate that decides PE-vs-BSS, and is refit one rung coarser on failure, stopping at the first rung that passes; if no rung passes it falls back to PE exactly as before. Every attempt is written to `ar_escalation_log.csv` with the verdict that triggered the next, so the resolution each component reports at becomes auditable rather than implicit in the config. Degenerate rungs (duplicate period count, or fewer than three periods) are dropped. **The cost is stated rather than buried:** every rung is a real multi-hour fit, and on this configuration the two capped components will burn their known-bad rungs first (shore pot-closure funnels at daily, ~1,165 divergences; boat diverged on ~100% of iterations at daily), so expect roughly 2-3x the wall clock, a 3-6 h run becoming 8-16 h. `ar_escalate_respect_cap = TRUE` is the cheap variant (escalate from the capped rung; no extra fits when the caps are right, but it cannot discover that a cap is too coarse). This does NOT replace the coverage rule, which answers a different question: coverage asks whether the series can identify a daily process at all, the ladder asks whether the sampler survives it.

**(8) OSP crab-only counts as a HARD LOWER BOUND on `f`.** OSP does not record combo trips (a boat that crabs AND fishes another fishery is labelled by the other fishery), so its crab-only share bounds `f` from below and is not an estimate of it. Treating it as an estimate would bias the boat harvest DOWN by exactly the combo rate, on precisely the halibut and tuna days where that rate is highest. The bound is therefore structural:

```text
f_lower[k]       ~ Beta(1,1)
osp_crab_only[i] ~ BetaBinomial(osp_total[i], f_lower[k(i)] * kappa, (1 - f_lower[k(i)]) * kappa)
f[k]             = f_lower[k] + (1 - f_lower[k]) * theta[k]
```

The likelihood is per OSP DAY and beta-binomial, not a binomial on the stratum sum. A binomial on the sum would treat every boat-day as an independent trial (~150 days x ~50 boats -> n ~ 7,500, `f_lower` SD ~ 0.005), which ~150 correlated daily observations do not support; `kappa` is estimated from the spread of the daily shares instead. Because the bound is HARD and `f` multiplies the boat linearly, an over-precise `f_lower` would push `f` up with unearned confidence - the same false-precision failure the weather module was rejected for.

`theta[k]` is the share of the NOT-crab-labelled boats that were also crabbing. **Identifiability, stated plainly: OSP alone identifies `f_lower`, never `theta`** - the counts cannot separate "few crab boats" from "many combo trips". `theta` rests on `crab_fraction_combo_share` (default 0.15, a PLACEHOLDER with exactly the standing `f = 0.3` had before the pilot) until the WPT/WBL egress classification covers the same stratum, at which point the egress Binomial `n_crab ~ Binomial(n_total, f)` pins `f` and hence `theta`. That is the operational reason to schedule egress classification days on days OSP is in port rather than treating the two as independent efforts. Degradation is EXACT, not approximate: with no OSP classification in a stratum, `f_lower` is pinned to 0, `f = theta`, and R hands Stan the ordinary `f` prior for `theta` instead of the combo prior, so the posterior is bit-identical to the pre-batch model. Both new terms sit on OBSERVED boat counts, never on the latent effort, so `f` stays in generated quantities only and the validated Phase 2/3 properties survive: the boat is exactly linear in `f`, model CPUE is invariant. **Ships `use_osp_crab_lower = FALSE`**, for the same reason everything else behaviour-changing here ships off: a TRUE default would move the boat harvest on the same run that first read the new column, and at `crab_fraction_strata = "none"` much of that move would come from the combo-share PLACEHOLDER rather than from OSP's data. It is additionally inert until the crab-only column exists in `WBL_boat_counts.xlsx` (`osp_crab_only_col`, default `WestportCrabOnlyEffort`), and a stratum below `crab_fraction_osp_min_obs` does not bind. The reader gains `f_lower_out` and `osp_f_kappa_out` alongside `f_crab_out`, so the bound, its day-to-day spread, and the level are reported separately.

**Also in the batch.** `B2_C`, `gamma_C`, `B_open`, `kappa_OSP`, `r_OSP`, `f_crab` and `f_lower` are added to the curated `structural_params_*` table, closing the standing Tier-4 item "surface `B2_C` in the curated report tables" (those terms were written only to `bss_full_summary_*`, so a reader had to open the full summary to see terms that move the estimate). The opener design matrix is passed to Stan FLAT (`X_open_flat`, length `D * K_open`) and rebuilt in `transformed data`, so the data block never contains a zero-extent matrix; that matches the marshalling pattern the empty effort streams already rely on. `06_diagnostics/test_improvements_2026-08-25.R` is a standalone, dependency-light harness (dplyr/tibble/tidyr/stringr/purrr only, no rstan) covering all of the above; run it before any long fit.

**Why it took six runs and an hour of wall clock to see a one-line error.** rstan does not raise a data-initialization failure as an R condition. It prints it to the MESSAGE stream, returns an EMPTY `stanfit` (mode 2), and lets the caller continue. The driver then degraded silently through three layers before dying somewhere else entirely:

| step | on an empty stanfit | visible symptom |
|---|---|---|
| `summary(fit, pars = bss_pars)$summary` | rstan's method returns `invisible(NULL)` for mode 2 | `bss_summary_*.csv` written as 3 bytes (`""`) |
| `write.csv(as.data.frame(NULL), ...)` | writes an empty frame | no error |
| `quantile(NULL, c(.025, .5, .975))` | `NA NA NA` | no error |
| `apply(rstan::extract(fit, "E")$E[, 1, , 1], 2, median)` | `NULL[, 1, , 1]` is `NULL`; `apply()` rejects it | **`dim(X) must have a positive length`** |

The chunk is `results='hide'`, so the one line that named the actual variable was the only diagnostic produced and the only one not shown.

**Fix (three parts, all on `OSP-boat-count-incorporation`).**

1. **The five fields are forwarded** in both `prep_bss_crab_pooled.R` and `prep_bss_crab_gear.R`. No model, prior or likelihood changes; with `use_osp_crab_lower = FALSE` they are `OSPF_n = 0` and three zero-length integer arrays, exactly as `crab_fraction_stan_data()`'s neutral path always intended. **The 2026-08-25 batch's behaviour is unchanged by this fix; it simply becomes runnable.**
2. **`03_R_functions/bss_stan_fit.R` (new).** Both drivers now call `bss_stan_fit()` instead of `rstan::stan()`. Same arguments, same seed, same RNG stream, so a fit that succeeds is unaffected and rung (i)'s bit-identity test on the gear track still means what it meant. It adds: `bss_assert_stan_data()`, which parses the `data` block of the `.stan` file being used and refuses to call the sampler when a declared variable is absent, non-numeric, or non-finite (this failure would now surface as `5 variable(s) declared in crab_bss_pooled.stan are missing from the data list: OSPF_n, ...` before any compile); `bss_assert_fit_usable()`, which rejects an empty `stanfit` and quotes what Stan actually said instead of letting `NULL` propagate; and a per-fit `stan_console_<label>_att<n>.log` in `output_dir`, recorded with a non-muffling `withCallingHandlers()` so sampler progress still reaches knitr, which keeps a chain failure diagnosable after a `results='hide'` knit.

## 5. Cross-cutting principles and lessons

- **Time-denominated effort is invalid for pot and trap gear.** Catch is sub-linear in soak time (saturation `beta` ~0.13-0.27), so crabber-hours and gear-hours both fail the linearity test; gear-deployments is the harvest-unbiased unit. The pipeline now measures this every run (`cpue_linearity_*.csv`, `cpue_saturation_*.csv`), so any new gear type or population must be run through those before its totals are trusted.
- **AR resolution is a real inference lever for sparse series, not a tuning knob.** Finer AR over-imputes effort in thinly sampled periods; the boat's weekly-vs-monthly choice moved its catch ~20% and was the entire pooled-vs-gear-resolved discrepancy. Cap AR at what the effort series can identify.
- **The harvest is summer-extrapolation-dominated.** The largest uncertainty and the largest BSS-vs-PE divergence both live in the thinly sampled months; no code refinement manufactures information that was not sampled. The single highest-value field change for 2025-26 is more summer interview/effort coverage.
- **BSS-vs-PE gaps are a diagnostic, not a defect, but demand a mechanism.** The two estimate the same harvest by different routes (design-based stratum expansion vs a latent process that imputes unobserved days and propagates uncertainty). A gap is expected where sampling under-covers effort. Report the BSS (now cross-model stable for the boat) with the PE as a caveated cross-check; the boat gap is mostly the PE undercounting (P0), the shore gap is an effort-imputation tied to `sigma_IE`.
- **Validate by run, never by reasoning alone (the "pin" lesson).** A change that looks inference-neutral on paper (the v7.3 pin; the B1.7 collapse) can perturb a delicate mass-matrix geometry. Isolate each change, compare against a confirmed baseline, and decide on pre-set criteria. Every "done pending run" item is not done until the run confirms it.

## 6. Field-protocol items for 2025-26 (not code)

These require sampling-design changes, not code, and should feed the field plan: **jetty and beach direct effort counts** (critique 3 / D3; shore effort currently proxied by dock gear counts); **minimum summer coverage targets by mode** (motivated by T1.1, the estimate's weakest point); **interview-time vs effort-count-time representativeness checks** (critique 10); and confirming effort-count and incomplete-trip protocols. Two items were added by the 2026-07-31 OSP work: **the WBL egress crab-vs-total classification pilot** (the crabbing fraction f is a 0.3 placeholder and the largest lever on the boat harvest; the pilot's `boats_crabbing` / `boats_total` counts, ideally stratified by month, replace it, and are the top open boat-credibility measurement, Tier 1); and **confirming the crab-creel trailer-count snapshot timing** (the production `osp_scale_is_tau = TRUE` correction assumes the trailer snapshot is taken at a representative instant, not a fixed daily peak; a peak-timed snapshot would make the ~2x turnover correction a floor rather than a point estimate, so this measurement bounds the boat's level). Spatial heterogeneity within the port (Float 20 vs 17-21, critique 11) is a design choice, not currently modeled.

**Added 2026-08-25.** Four field/ops items, in priority order:

1. **Ask OSP for a daily crab-ONLY boat count** alongside the existing daily private-boat total. The code path is built and inert (2026-08-25 update, item 8). Be explicit with OSP about what is being asked for and why: because combo trips are labelled by the non-crab fishery, the count is a LOWER BOUND on crabbing boats, and that is exactly how the model uses it. If OSP can additionally flag combo trips, even on a sample of days, that would identify the combo share directly and is worth more than more crab-only days.
2. **Schedule the WPT/WBL egress classification on days OSP is in port.** This is the single highest-value scheduling change available. The OSP crab-only stream identifies the lower bound `f_lower` but can never identify the combo share `theta`; only the egress classification (which counts a combo boat as crabbing) can. Overlapping strata pin both; disjoint strata leave `theta` on a placeholder prior with the same standing `f = 0.3` had. The pilot should still classify by month and day-type, and per stratum, not just in aggregate.
3. **Sample Sundays.** Sunday is sampled on 6 of 50 shore days and 5 of 50 boat days, and Monday on 2 of 51 and 3 of 51. Friday could be moved out of the weekend stratum from existing data; Sunday cannot be characterised without a shift change. Eight sampled Sundays gets within 0.5 CV points of sampling all 52 (Tier 2). Sundays carry 13.7% of shore and 16.6% of boat season catch.
4. **More in-season shore I/E days.** Only 6 fall inside the season, and they skew low-effort (mean percentile 0.27, rank test p = 0.06) against the ~40 a season Pollock et al. (1997) would imply. This sets the turnover `tau_shore` that ~80% of the shore expansion rests on, and after the 2026-08-25 unit fix the stream can finally inform it rather than fight the gear counts.
